OCD in Color is a 501(c)(3) registered nonprofit that aims to raise awareness about obsessive-compulsive disorder through art. Moreover, it seeks to improve access to mental health care for individuals with OCD by providing financial assistance for therapy sessions.

Who We Are

Color is the whole point

OCD is often misunderstood and reduced to stereotypes. We believe every experience with OCD is personal and complex. This is why we use art, education and storytelling to make these experiences visible.

🎨
Art as Advocacy
We use creative expression to share different perspectives on OCD, whether it is drawing, painting, music, dance, photography, animation ...
🔬
Research with Community
Our research team explores existing medical and scientific resources about OCD to spread accurate information, correct misconceptions and raise awareness.
🤝
Peer Connection
Our network connects individuals diagnosed with OCD with therapists, support groups, and guidance that recognizes the challenges of living with OCD.

Symmetry OCD :
Chloe Park · 2026
Symmetry OCD is a certain subtype of OCD where people may feel distress when things are not adjusted perfectly or in order.
Journal of Anxiety Disorders
In everyday life they may feel the need to check to make sure everything is perfect or symmetrical. This cycle is done to relieve anxiety, although it may not make logical sense. For example, they may have everyday thoughts such as, “This is not in order, I need to fix this” . Another example includes written words, where a person feels distressed when words are not correctly on the page or shaped imperfectly. Common misconceptions about symmetry OCD is that it just means someone is a “ perfectionist” , and is a personality trait. It is an actual disorder that can affect peoples’ day to day lives drastically. It is not something done for fun or someone’s personal preference, but rather because it causes distress and anxiety to that person.

OCD myths
Nivedita, 2026
Obsessive compulsive disorder is a long lasting disorder which gives a person uncontrollable and recurring thoughts(obsessions),makes them engage in repetitive behaviors(compulsions) or both. People with this disorder can have symptoms that consume their time or interfere with their daily life. Yet poeple confuse its symptoms with other myths.
Transcultural Psychiatry
COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS:- -❌OCD is a minor personality quirk or preference ✅OCD is more than just a quirk or preference and is a real mental health condition that causes unwanted thoughts for people diagnosed with it. -❌compulsions are always physical actions. ✅compulsions refer to thoughts or behaviors that a person with OCD uses to get rid of obsessions and reduce anxiety. -❌OCD is rare and doesn’t affect many people. ✅OCD can affect people of all ages,sex,and backgrounds. It can develop as early as preschool. -❌OCD is just a phase and will go away on its own. ✅OCD is a real mental health disorder that requires treatment and diagnosis under the guidance of a licensed healthcare practitioner. -❌OCD is caused by a weak charecter or lack of willpower. ✅people with OCD struggle with unwanted thoughts and behaviors which means it takes more willpower to overcome this condition.

Myths about OCD
Himanshi Mechu, 2026
I.Everyone has a little bit of OCD- While some of us may feel anxious from time to time, an OCD diagnosis is very different. The obsessions and compulsions one experiences from OCD can significantly impact their life. Comparing everyday habits to the distress experienced by those with OCD trivializes the severity and impact of the disorder.
Behavior Therapy
II.People with OCD are just anxious or neurotic- Someone with anxiety may experience intrusive thoughts and develop coping skills or self-soothing habits to alleviate their anxiety, and some of that is normal. But they don’t experience the same intensity of obsessions and compulsions that can take over daily life. III.OCD is just about being overly clean or organized- While some people with OCD may have compulsions related to cleanliness or orderliness, OCD can manifest in various forms. Common obsessions are fear-related, like fears about making mistakes, forgetting things or losing control of their behavior. Importantly, you might not always have the same obsessions—they can change over your lifetime. IV.OCD is untreatable- OCD is a treatable condition, and seeking professional help can significantly improve the management of symptoms and help individuals lead more fulfilling lives.
Sources:
  • The National Library Of Medecine
  • UNI ScholarWorks
  • Jefferson Health

OCD symptoms
Himanshi Mechu, 2026
Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD) is a neurobiological disorder. It is classified as an anxiety disorder that forces a person into a series of repetitive thoughts, actions, and behaviors that need to be performed to decrease anxiety. A person with obsessions tries to control them by engaging in compulsive behavior. The thoughts, impulses, urges, or images compel their way into an individual's thinking. OCD patients agree on the functional value of rituals. Their value is to reduce the anxiety generated by the obsessions.
Clinical Psychology Review
I.Washing hands excessively or in a certain way II.Excessive showering, bathing, tooth-brushing, grooming, or toilet routines III.Cleaning household items or other objects excessively IV.Mental review of events to prevent harm (to oneself others, to prevent terrible consequences) V.Praying to prevent harm (to oneself others, to prevent terrible consequences) VI.Counting while performing a task to end on a "good," "right," or "safe" number VII.Putting things in order or arranging things until it "feels right" VIII.Telling, asking, or confessing to get reassurance IX.Avoiding situations that might trigger your obsessions X.Routine activities (e.g., going in or out doors, getting up or down from chairs) XI.Body movements (e.g., tapping, touching, blinking) Activities in "multiples" (e.g., doing a task three times because three is a "good," "right," "safe"
